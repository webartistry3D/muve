🗒️ Breaking Down Bolt Fare
Your trip is approximately 24.2 kilometers long and takes a minimum of 44 minutes under light traffic. Bolt calculates your fare by combining four distinct charges:

- The Base Fare: A flat starting fee (typically around ₦400 – ₦500) just for requesting the ride.
- The Distance Charge (Per Km): At roughly ₦145 per km, the 24.2 km distance alone accounts for about ₦3,500.
- The Time Charge (Per Minute): Bolt charges around ₦25 to ₦30 for every minute you are in the car. If your trip takes 44 minutes, that adds ₦1,320. If you get stuck in notorious Lagos traffic (e.g., around Eko Bridge or Third Mainland approaches) and the trip stretches to 1.5 hours, the time charge alone jumps to nearly ₦3,000.
- Surge Pricing (The Multiplier): Because Festac to Ikoyi is a heavy commuter route—especially during morning rush hours—demand frequently outstrips available drivers. Bolt automatically applies a surge multiplier (e.g., 1.4x to 2.0x) to the entire bill, easily pushing a baseline ₦5,300 trip up into the ₦8,000 to ₦10,000 bracket


inDrive does not use a rigid, hidden math formula to force a final price on you like Bolt does. Instead, when you input Festac to Ikoyi, the app uses its algorithm to calculate a minimum recommended baseline fare—usually around ₦4,500 to ₦5,500 for that distance—which then serves as the starting point for human bargaining.

Because inDrive only takes a ~10% commission from drivers (compared to Bolt’s hefty 25%–30%), the baseline rates are structurally lower. Here is exactly how that ₦5,500 recommended fare breaks down and plays out:

The "Human Traffic" Variable (No Traffic Auto-Billing):Unlike Bolt, which automatically tracks GPS delays and bills you ₦30 for every single extra minute you sit on Eko Bridge, inDrive locks in your price before you move.
